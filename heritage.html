Why Japan?

When it comes to precision metalwork, hermetic sealing, and the patience to do something right the first time — Japan has no equal. Here's what Japanese craftsmanship means for your Rion piece.

i

Precision Without Compromise

Japanese craft culture is built on the principle of shokunin — a master craftsperson who dedicates their life to a single skill. At Rion, each jewel passes through hands trained specifically for this work, measured to fractions of a millimeter.

ii

Materials of the Highest Grade

We use K18 gold and Pt900 platinum sourced and graded to Japanese JIS standards — among the most rigorous material standards in the world. The metal itself is the quality benchmark.

iii

A Sealing Technique That Lasts

The hermetic sealing process at Rion has been refined through years of iteration. An airtight chamber drilled and capped in the same precious metal as the jewel — designed to hold your hair intact for generations without degradation.

iv

Accountability from Start to Finish

Unlike mass-market memorial products, every Rion piece is made by identifiable hands, documented with photographs, and backed by a lifetime polishing guarantee. You know exactly who made yours — and so do we.

A tradition centuries old.

Memorial jewelry predates almost every modern tradition you can think of. It has been worn by royalty and everyday families alike, across cultures, faiths, and continents.

1500s – 1700s

European Mourning Rings

Hair-filled rings and lockets were commonly exchanged among nobility and families in England, France, and Germany as tokens of love and loss — the earliest precursors of modern memorial jewelry.

Victorian Era, 1837 – 1901

Queen Victoria's Influence

After Prince Albert's death in 1861, Queen Victoria wore his hair in a locket for the rest of her life. Memorial jewelry became a cornerstone of Victorian mourning culture, worn by millions across Britain and America.

20th Century

A Quiet Tradition

As mass production dominated jewelry, memorial pieces became rarer but never disappeared. Families passed them down, artisans kept the craft alive, and the need to carry someone close never went away.

Today

The Rion Generation

Modern memorial jewelry has returned — refined by precision metalwork, hermetic sealing, and global shipping. Rion is the next chapter of this story: Japanese craft, brought to the world.

Why hair?

Of everything a person leaves behind, hair is uniquely durable, uniquely personal, and uniquely theirs.

i

DNA, Preserved Forever

Hair contains the genetic signature of the person. It is not a symbol or a representation — it is literally them, sealed inside the metal you wear every day.

ii

Does Not Decay

Properly sealed, hair lasts for centuries without degradation. Ancient Egyptian mummies still hold their hair after 4,000 years. Sealed inside solid gold or platinum, yours will too.

iii

Hygienic & Safe

Hair is keratin — a stable, non-reactive protein. It carries no bodily fluid, no contamination, and no health risk. The sealed chamber is airtight, permanent, and completely inert.

iv

Respected Across Traditions

Hair-sealed memorial jewelry is widely accepted across Christian, Buddhist, Shinto, Hindu, and many other traditions. We approach every order with openness and respect.
